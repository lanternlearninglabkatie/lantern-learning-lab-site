Home education or PEP: which one are you?

Home education and PEP are two different legal ways to meet Florida's school attendance law, and you can only be in one at a time. If you're already homeschooling, deciding whether to add or switch to PEP is a roughly $8,000-a-year decision, and families get this wrong more often than you'd think.

Why they're not the same thing

Florida Statute 1003.01(16) lists six ways to meet the attendance requirement. Two of them are:

  • (d) a home education program
  • (f) a personalized education program (PEP)

These are listed as separate options — not two names for the same thing, and not something you can be enrolled in at once.

What each one actually requires of you

Home educationPEP
Letter of intent to your districtRequired, within 30 daysNot required
Annual requirementAn evaluation (five methods to choose from)A norm-referenced test, results to Step Up
Who pays for servicesYou, out of pocket (unless you also hold FES-UA)Step Up, from your PEP scholarship account
Ending itFile a notice of termination with a final evaluation, within 30 daysStep Up's own switching rules

If you're moving from home education to PEP

Florida law requires you to file a notice of termination and one last annual evaluation within 30 days of ending home education (FS 1002.41(1)(c)) — on top of whatever Step Up asks you to do to start PEP. This is the single most missed step families run into.

What about FES-UA?

FES-UA (Unique Abilities) scholarships are different again: FES-UA families stay in home education. To receive FES-UA funding while homeschooling, you must have filed a letter of intent and keep it active — so everything on this site about home education (the letter of intent, the portfolio, the annual evaluation) still applies to you. The good news: your annual evaluation is a cost your FES-UA scholarship can pay for directly, so it doesn't come out of pocket.

Which test does my PEP child need?

PEP students need a norm-referenced test each year, not an evaluation. A guide to choosing the right one is coming soon to this site.

What usually goes wrong

The mistake isn't picking the wrong one — it's not realizing you have to formally leave one before starting the other. A family can't simply start using PEP funds while their letter of intent sits active with the district; Florida and Step Up both expect you to close out home education properly, with a final evaluation, before PEP funding continues.

The other common surprise: families assume PEP is "better" because the dollar amount is bigger, without checking whether their child actually needs — or wants — to leave the flexibility of a self-directed home education program for PEP's structure. Neither is better; they fit different families.
